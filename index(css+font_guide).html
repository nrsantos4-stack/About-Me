My Advocacy

Me using a turntable.

I advocate for increased engagement with vinyl records among younger generations as a way to reconnect
with not only older generations but also with history.

With the rise of digital music piracy, many music artists are leaning towards vinyl as a countermeasure.

The phonograph record became a way to:

  • Reassert value in music as a physical, collectible product
  • Engage directly with fans seeking tangible, high-quality formats
  • Generate new revenue streams amid falling digital/streaming income

The advantages vinyl has over digital formats in resisting plagiarism are:

  • Tangible Ownership - Fans want something real — artwork, liner notes, exclusivity. Pirates can’t duplicate physical appeal.
  • Audiophile Quality - Vinyl offers analog warmth and depth that MP3s (especially pirated ones) lack.
  • Limited Editions - Special pressings, colored vinyl, and numbered copies create scarcity and value.
  • Merch Culture - Vinyl is often bundled with merchandise, concert tickets, or autographs — making it part of the fan experience.
  • In-Person Sales - Many vinyl records are sold at concerts or indie stores — harder for pirates to replicate or disrupt.

Due to their tangible nature, vinyl records have come to symbolize a myriad of attitudes and perceptions.

Some of the things they represent are:

  • Artistic integrity
  • Human connection
  • Material culture
  • Deliberate listening
  • Resistance to commodification
